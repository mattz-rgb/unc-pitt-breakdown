The result in brief

North Carolina beat No. 25 Pitt 21-17 on Saturday behind a 75 yard touchdown from Billy Edwards Jr. to Shamar Easter, a defense that closed the game with a run of fourth down stops, and a second half in which the Tar Heels outscored the Panthers 14-7 after trailing 10-7 at the break. It is UNC's first win over a ranked team since beating No. 25 Miami in 2023, its first road win over a ranked team since beating No. 9 Miami in 2020, and the first win over a ranked opponent of Bill Belichick's college head coaching career. It ended Pitt's unbeaten start at 5-0 and moved UNC to 3-2 overall and 1-1 in the ACC with a trip to Duke next.

The box score says UNC should have lost. Pitt had 369 yards to UNC's 269, 150 rushing yards to 20, 23 first downs to 13, 7 third down conversions to 3, and held the ball for 36:32 to UNC's 23:28. From the snap counts, Pitt ran roughly 86 plays to UNC's 52 (the two UNC defensive backs who played every snap, Jaiden Patterson and Peyton Waters, were on the field for 86). The Tar Heels won because they were far more efficient with the plays that mattered. UNC's three touchdown drives took 16 plays and 6:38 combined, while Pitt's three scoring drives took 33 plays and 14:46. Two completions, 49 yards to Jordan Shipp and 75 to Easter, produced 124 of UNC's 269 yards, which means the other 50 offensive plays netted 145. And while each team threw two interceptions, Pitt turned both of Edwards's into zero points, one erased by a Greg Smith interception in the end zone and the other by a failed fourth down at the UNC 21.

Edwards finished 18 of 26 for 249 yards, two touchdowns and two interceptions. Pitt's Holden Geriner, making the first start of his college career, went 27 of 44 for 219 yards, no touchdowns and two interceptions, an average of 5.0 yards per attempt. La'Vell Wright scored both Pitt touchdowns on the ground, and Demon June scored UNC's lone rushing touchdown.

How the game went

First Quarter: Pitt Does What the Preview Said It Would

Pitt took the opening kickoff and ran a 12 play, 75 yard drive that lasted 5:11 and included eight rushes for 53 yards. A pass interference penalty on Smith in the end zone set up Wright's 2 yard touchdown run, and the Panthers had a 7-0 lead. UNC had no answer in the first 15 minutes. At the end of the quarter Pitt was outgaining the Tar Heels 96-25, even though Geriner was only 6 of 9 for 35 yards, because the Panthers were getting what they wanted on the ground. DeMarco Ward sacked Edwards in the quarter. UNC's defense was also playing without two starters, Isaiah Johnson and Jaylen Harvey, and the lack of a pass rush showed early with Geriner finding open throws.

Second Quarter: A Quick Punch Back

Pitt stretched the lead to 10-0 with a 14 play, 67 yard drive that took 7:00 and ended in a 41 yard Antonio Chadha field goal. By the middle of the second quarter, Pitt had run 26 plays and used 12:11 of clock to score 10 points. UNC then showed the quick strike ability that decided the game. Edwards opened the next drive with a 49 yard completion to Shipp and finished the five play, 75 yard march in 2:14 with a 7 yard touchdown pass to Jelani Thurman, cutting it to 10-7. Pitt missed a field goal in the final 30 seconds of the half, and the Panthers took a 10-7 lead into the locker room despite owning most of the half's statistics.

Third Quarter: Three Interceptions and the First Lead

The first 15 plays of the second half held three interceptions in 6:06. Pitt's Cole Woodson picked off Edwards on UNC's opening possession, handing Pitt the ball at the UNC 31. A UNC blitz then forced Geriner into an interception in the end zone by Smith, his third of the season, and it was still 10-7. Tre Miller picked off Geriner as well, and UNC put together a 10 play, 51 yard drive that took 4:13 and ended with June's 1 yard touchdown run with 22 seconds left in the quarter. That made it 14-10, UNC's first lead of the game. It was also the first third quarter points Pitt had allowed all season; through five games the Panthers had outscored opponents 42-0 in the period.

Fourth Quarter: The Swing Plays

Pitt answered with a 7 play, 75 yard drive that took 2:35, helped by a 40 yard catch by Malik Knight, and Wright scored his second short touchdown with 12:47 left for a 17-14 lead. On the first snap of UNC's next possession, Edwards hit Easter on a deep inside post and the tight end took it 75 yards for the go ahead touchdown, a score that took 11 seconds of game clock. After a defensive stop and a punt, Edwards was pressured with 6:31 left, threw the ball up to avoid a sack, and it went off Shipp's fingertips into the hands of Pitt's Shadarian Harrison, who returned it 22 yards into UNC territory. Pitt drove to the UNC 21, and on fourth and 2 Geriner sailed a throw over a wide open Cataurus Hicks. With the game in the final two minutes, Pitt faced fourth and 2 again, this time from the UNC 31, and Derek McDonald punched the ball loose as Wright went to the ground short of the line, giving UNC the ball with 1:51 left. UNC's official recap says the defense held Pitt on three consecutive fourth down attempts to end the game, and the final Pitt possession ended with four straight incompletions.

By the numbers

The team statistics are lopsided in Pitt's direction: 369 yards to 269, 219 passing yards for Pitt to 249 for UNC, 150 rushing yards to 20, 23 first downs to 13, and 42 penalty yards for Pitt to 22 for UNC. UNC averaged 5.2 yards per play (269 on 52 snaps) and Pitt 4.3 (369 on roughly 86). The 20 yards on the ground is the official net figure, which includes sack yardage, so the backs themselves did better than that: Benjamin Hall had 25 yards on 12 carries, per Inside Carolina. Pitt had been allowing 55.6 rushing yards per game, so even a 20 yard day is a result of a very good run defense and a line that allowed three sacks, not simply a bad day by the running backs.

Edwards's season line now stands at 79 of 132 (59.8 percent) for 1,068 yards, eight touchdowns and five interceptions. Four of those five interceptions have come in the last two weeks, which was the specific concern Belichick raised afterward. Shipp topped 90 yards for the second straight week (109 against Notre Dame, 93 here), and Thurman's touchdown was his fourth of the season. Easter has a touchdown catch in each of the last two games, and he played only nine snaps against Pitt.

The defense was the most active unit in the building. There were 98 total tackles for UNC defenders, with Xavier Lewis and McDonald each making 12, and each with a tackle for loss. Hoilette had a sack, and Lewis and Timir Hickman-Collins combined on another, so UNC finished with two to Pitt's three. The workload was heavy: Jaiden Patterson went from 70 snaps against Notre Dame to 86 against Pitt, Peyton Waters from 69 to 86, Greg Smith from 65 to 84, Lewis from 63 to 73, Melkart Abou Jaoude from 42 to 72, and McDonald from 64 to 71.

The snap counts also show how UNC lined up. Adding up the defensive groups gives 4.06 linemen, 1.99 linebackers and 4.95 defensive backs per play, which means UNC was in its 4-2-5 (nickel) look on essentially every snap, with edge players counted as linemen. On offense, the skill snaps add up to exactly 260, or five per play over 52 plays: running backs 57, tight ends 79 and wide receivers 124. That works out to a second tight end on roughly 27 of the 52 snaps, a little more than half the game, with Thurman on the field for all 52. Easter's 75 yard touchdown, on only his ninth snap, came out of that heavy personnel, which is the kind of play action shot over the middle that the preview said the tight end seam would produce.

Where UNC won the game

The defense finished the game

The game came down to the final ten minutes, and UNC's defense won them. After Pitt took its 17-14 lead, the Tar Heels forced a punt, then three consecutive turnovers on downs. Those came on a high throw to a wide open Hicks at the UNC 21, a fumble knocked loose by McDonald on fourth and 2 at the UNC 31, and the final drive of four incompletions. This was a defense that had allowed Notre Dame to run for 241 yards and Clemson roughly 186 in its previous two games. Pitt still ran for 150 against it, and the Panthers moved the ball in the first half, but UNC's second half tackling was clearly better. The defense began to wrap up on first contact after halftime and produced a pair of sacks, and Belichick credited an improved job of setting the edge and not letting the ball get outside. Given Johnson and Harvey were out and nickel back Kaleb Cost left early with an injury, it was a better effort than the roster suggested it should be.

Two explosive plays decided the offense

UNC's offense did not move the ball consistently. It gained 269 yards on 52 plays, rushed for 20, and punted or turned the ball over on most drives that did not end in a touchdown. But it created two huge plays, the 49 yard completion to Shipp that started the second quarter touchdown drive and the 75 yard score to Easter, and those plays were 46 percent of the offense's yardage. Belichick said afterward that Pitt plays aggressively, tries to hit for a loss and knock the ball behind the sticks, and that this makes the defense vulnerable if you execute, and that UNC's team speed turned shorter plays into longer ones. Edwards said every play is designed to be an 80 yard touchdown, and that it was nice to see Easter catch and run with this one. That is a different offensive identity than the run first plan on paper, and it is one that worked against the ACC's second best run defense.

UNC answered every punch

Pitt had three scoring drives and UNC had an immediate response to two of them. After Pitt went up 10-0, UNC marched 75 yards in five plays and 2:14. After Pitt retook the lead at 17-14, UNC scored in one play and 11 seconds. Between those points, UNC also survived a bad stretch to start the second half, when Edwards threw an interception at his own 31 yard line and the defense had to protect a 10-7 lead. The Tar Heels never trailed by more than 10, and after both Pitt's field goal and Pitt's fourth quarter touchdown they scored on their very next possession.

The turnover swing

UNC and Pitt each threw two interceptions, but the effect was not equal. Woodson's interception gave Pitt the ball at the UNC 31 and Harrison's return put Pitt in UNC territory, and the Panthers got zero points from either. On the first, the defense called a blitz that forced Geriner into Smith's end zone interception, which preserved the 10-7 score. On the second, Pitt got inside the 21 and still turned the ball over on downs. Meanwhile, Smith's and Miller's interceptions came in the third quarter when UNC needed to flip the game, and UNC turned the stretch into the touchdown that took the lead. Belichick said afterward that UNC wanted to get its interception total down to zero, and that this was about the entire offense and not just the quarterback, so the margin was uncomfortably thin even though the outcome held.

Pressure and coverage on a first time starter

The preview said the best defensive outcome was getting Geriner into third and long and forcing a first time starter to win with his arm. It took a half to happen, but it did. Geriner was 6 of 9 for 35 yards in the first quarter and still finished with only 5.0 yards per attempt, two interceptions and four straight incompletions to end the game. He missed Hicks high on fourth down, and UNC made him uncomfortable throughout the second half. The 27 completions on 44 attempts show that Pitt got a lot of easy throws, but nothing downfield. Knight's 40 yard catch on the touchdown drive is the longest Pitt completion I could find.

Depth, adjustments and special teams

UNC got serious contributions from backups. Ade Willie played 60 snaps after Cost left early (Cost finished with 8 snaps), and the radio broadcast repeatedly praised his coverage. Hoilette played 55 snaps in place of Harvey with a sack, Nicco Maggio played 42 in place of Johnson, and Calvin Thomas played 37. Special teams were also solid. Belichick specifically pointed to the kickoff coverage unit, noting that Pitt tried to bring one out and UNC stopped it, Aeron Burrell had a 47 yard punt, and Rece Verhoff made all three extra points. UNC also committed 20 fewer penalty yards than Pitt, although a pass interference penalty on Smith helped set up the opening score.

Areas for improvement

The run game has disappeared

UNC ran for 20 net yards against Pitt, 77 against Notre Dame, 214 at Clemson and 90 against TCU. The offense that was designed to run the football physically is winning with the pass, and Saturday's result is not a plan you can repeat every week. Hall carried 12 times for 25 yards (2.1 per carry), June scored from a yard out, and UNC used a second tight end on roughly half its snaps and still could not generate push. Part of that is Pitt's front, which came in allowing 55.6 yards per game, but part of it is the offensive line, which allowed three sacks (the sack yardage is counted against the team's rushing total), did not use left tackle Jordan Hall (he does not appear in the snap counts), and used the same five linemen on all 52 snaps. The preview said a run first team that gets stuffed becomes a pass first team on long yardage, and that is exactly what happened. The reason UNC could survive it is that Edwards and the receivers hit two explosive plays, which is a lot to count on.

Turnovers are still a problem

Edwards now has five interceptions this season, four of them in the last two games, and Belichick said the goal is zero and that the Tar Heels cannot win consistently while turning it over. Neither interception on Saturday cost UNC points, but only because the defense and Pitt's mistakes cooperated. The first, by Woodson, came on UNC's opening possession of the second half and gave Pitt the ball at the UNC 31, and the Panthers were a touchdown away from a 17-7 lead. The second came with 6:31 left and UNC ahead 21-17: Edwards, pressured, threw the ball up to avoid a sack, it deflected off Shipp's fingertips, and Harrison returned it 22 yards into UNC territory. Throwing it away or taking the sack is the safer decision at that moment, and a more precise Pitt offense scores there. This is the most likely way UNC loses a close game in the next few weeks.

Slow starts and early tackling

UNC trailed 10-0, was outgained 96-25 in the first quarter and allowed a 12 play, 75 yard touchdown drive on the opening possession, with eight rushes for 53 yards, plus a pass interference penalty on Smith in the end zone. The defense struggled tackling early and did not generate pressure, and Geriner found open receivers without difficulty. The defense was missing Johnson and Harvey, which explains part of it, but UNC cannot count on being 10 points down and hoping to adjust, especially on the road at Duke next week.

Third downs, time of possession and workload

Pitt converted 7 third downs, got 23 first downs, held the ball for 36:32 and ran about 86 plays. UNC's offense produced only 52 plays, and the snap counts show the defense paying for it. Patterson and Waters played all 86 snaps, Smith played 84, Lewis played 73, Abou Jaoude played 72 and McDonald played 71. That workload held up against Pitt, but it will not keep holding up if UNC continues to be a team that wins with a few quick strikes while its offense runs fewer than 55 plays. Fatigue is a real risk, especially with Johnson and Harvey out and the heaviest workloads going to Lewis, Abou Jaoude and McDonald.

Injuries and depth

Johnson and Harvey were out, Cost left early with an injury (Belichick said he got hurt running into the camera operators on the sideline, and complained about player safety), and Hall, who was questionable all week, did not play. Willie, Hoilette, Maggio, Thomas and the backup linemen stepped in well, but I do not know the status of any of those players for Duke, and the next three games (Duke, Syracuse and No. 4 Miami) will test depth in a way Pitt did not. Pass rush is the area most dependent on getting Johnson and Harvey back.

How the preview held up

My preview picked UNC 27, Pitt 24, an upset pick against a four point spread, and UNC won by four. That part held, and so did several of the core arguments: the quarterback gap mattered, Geriner was uncomfortable once UNC adjusted, the tight end seam produced a game winning touchdown, and the second half fade did not repeat, with UNC outscoring Pitt 14-7 after the break and allowing only one second half touchdown.
